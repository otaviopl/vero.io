# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

B2B commercial teams: SDRs, BDRs, and commercial intelligence leads doing outbound prospecting. They work on desktop during the workday, moving fast between lead sources, CRM syncs, and enrichment workflows. They are not technical users, but they are expert users of commercial tools. Their job is to generate qualified pipeline, and every second of friction costs them deals.

## Product Purpose

Vero.io unifies lead capture, data enrichment, Apollo credit traceability, and HubSpot sync into one operational pipeline. It replaces the fragmented stack (extension + spreadsheet + manual CRM) with a cohesive environment where every data point has a known origin, a preserved original version, and a tracked enrichment cost. Success means an SDR can go from a LinkedIn profile to an enriched, synced CRM contact without leaving the platform or losing the audit trail.

## Brand Personality

Precise. Tactical. No-nonsense.

The interface earns trust through competence, not charisma. It does not explain itself; it demonstrates itself. It feels like a well-engineered instrument, not a marketing brochure. The tone is direct: Portuguese-language product for Brazilian commercial teams, professional register without stiffness.

## References

Stripe, Brex, Mercury: fintech-grade confidence. Data-heavy but polished. Command-and-control energy. They present complexity without apology, and they make dense information feel organized rather than overwhelming. Their interfaces say "we understand this domain deeply" before a word is read.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS neutrality: Notion-cream backgrounds, Linear-minimalism for its own sake, the kind of "clean and modern" that is indistinguishable from 200 other B2B tools. Vero should be immediately recognizable. A screenshot of any screen should read as Vero, not as "a B2B SaaS."

Also avoid: neon-on-black hacker aesthetics (alienates commercial users), overdesigned glassmorphism (decoration without function), and enterprise gray (Salesforce weight without Salesforce brand equity).

## Design Principles

1. **The interface demonstrates the product.** The landing page does not describe what Vero does — it shows it. Data structures, pipeline flows, and live feeds are visual arguments, not illustrations.
2. **Precision over decoration.** Every color, weight, and spacing decision must carry information. If it could be removed without losing meaning, it should be.
3. **Context is never buried.** The audit trail, credit cost, and data origin are always a single glance away. Nothing important requires drilling.
4. **Confidence through restraint.** Authority comes from what is not there. No badge inflation, no progress-indicator theater, no animated counters that don't reflect real state.
5. **Speed is respect.** The interface moves as fast as the users do. Interactions confirm immediately; loading states are brief and purposeful.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard best practices. No specific WCAG audit target. Ensure keyboard navigation works for primary flows and color alone is never the only signal carrier (always pair with shape, label, or position).
